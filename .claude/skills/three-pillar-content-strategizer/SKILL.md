# Skill 3: Three-Pillar Content Strategizer

Part of the HubSpot for Startups *5 Claude Skills to Crush Founder-Led Content* system.

This skill runs in two modes. Setup mode helps a founder define three content pillars specific to their business, their buyer, and their goals. The output is a reusable pillar document they can reference every time they decide what to create. Scoring mode takes a content idea or draft and scores it against those pillars. It tells the founder which pillar the idea serves, how strongly, and whether it's worth their time.

The pillars solve two problems founders consistently run into. Without them, founders either post whatever comes to mind with no strategic coherence across their content, or they stall trying to decide if something is worth publishing and nothing goes out. Defined pillars create a filter. The filter does the deciding.

---

## Mode detection

Check for a pillar document before doing anything else.

**No pillar document present:** Run setup mode. Do not attempt to score anything without pillars to score against.

**Pillar document present + a content idea or draft:** Run scoring mode.

**Pillar document present + no idea to score:** Ask what the founder wants to evaluate.

If it is unclear which mode applies, ask one question: "Do you have a pillar document, or do you need to build one first?"

---

## Setup mode

### What to collect from the founder

Ask for the following. Do not present these as a numbered list or a form. Ask conversationally, and allow for incomplete or rough answers.

**Their buyer.** Not a demographic profile. The actual person who signs the contract or makes the purchasing decision. What their role is, what they are actively trying to solve, what a bad quarter looks like for them. The more specific this is, the more useful the pillars will be.

**What their company does.** Not the pitch deck version. What problem they solve, for whom, and what specifically changes for the buyer when the product works.

**What they want content to accomplish.** Pipeline generation, recruiting, investor visibility, brand in a new market, or some combination. They need to name which business outcomes matter most, because the pillars should map directly to those outcomes. If they say "all of them," push back. Ask which one failing would hurt most.

**Optionally:** Examples of their own past content that performed well, and examples from other founders or companies they wish they had written. Both reveal what resonates in their space and what register they are aiming for.

If a founder provides answers that are vague or generic (for example, "my buyer is decision-makers at mid-market companies"), ask one follow-up to get specific. "What does that person's title usually look like, and what would make their week worse?"

### What to produce

Three defined pillars. Each pillar requires all six components below.

---

**1. Name**

Short, specific, and memorable. It should mean something to this founder about this business. Generic category labels (Thought Leadership, Educational, Conversion) are rejected. A stranger reading the name should have a reasonable guess at what the pillar covers without needing to read the description.

---

**2. Description**

One paragraph. What this pillar covers, why it exists for this specific business, and what it is doing in the content mix. Write it with enough precision that a stranger could read it and correctly sort ten content ideas into this pillar or out of it without asking the founder any questions. If the description is so broad that anything could fit, it needs to be tightened.

---

**3. Audience**

Who reads this content. Not a demographic summary. Describe the specific person, their awareness level, and where they are in relation to buying, hiring, or following. A Signal-type pillar (top of funnel) reaches a different person than a Proof-type pillar (bottom of funnel). Name that difference.

---

**4. Business outcome**

What this pillar is doing for the company. Be direct: builds audience with future buyers, generates pipeline, closes deals already in motion, attracts engineering talent, creates investor awareness. One or two outcomes per pillar. Not all pillars do the same thing, and that is by design.

---

**5. Example topics or angles**

Three to five examples drawn from the founder's specific business context. Not generic examples that could belong to any startup. These should be specific enough that the founder could write a post from any one of them without asking a follow-up question.

---

**6. What does NOT belong here**

Explicit boundaries. Name two or three types of content that might seem like a fit but are not. This matters as much as the definition. Without it, the pillar expands to absorb everything and loses its filtering function.

---

### The default framework

Use Signal / Pain / Proof as scaffolding. Adapt it to the founder's situation. Rename the pillars. Adjust the definitions. Restructure the model entirely if their situation calls for it.

**Signal (audience-building).** Expert takes on trends, shifts, and emerging problems in the founder's space. Reaches people who are not buying today but follow because the founder sees things others miss. Top of funnel. The reader is aware of the space but not actively shopping.

**Pain (pipeline-building).** Content written directly for the ideal buyer, addressing problems they recognize, in language they already use. This is where followers start thinking the founder actually understands their world. Middle of funnel. The reader is aware of the problem and evaluating options.

**Proof (deal-closing).** Case studies, product decisions with context, results with real numbers, and stories from the specific world the buyer lives in. Does the selling before the sales call happens. Bottom of funnel. The reader is aware of the product and deciding.

The framework is a starting point, not a template to copy. A founder selling to enterprise CIOs has different pillars than one selling to SMB marketing teams. A founder whose primary goal is recruiting needs a different mix than one focused on pipeline. A B2C founder may not have a "deal-closing" pillar at all. The framework serves the founder. Adjust accordingly.

If the founder's stated priority is pipeline, the Pain pillar should anchor the document. If recruiting is the stated priority, one pillar may focus entirely on culture, team, and what it is like to build at this company. Follow the business outcome, not the template.

### Format of the pillar document output

Produce the pillar document in a clean, readable format the founder can save, paste elsewhere, and hand off to a writer or agency. Use section headers for each pillar. Within each pillar, use labeled fields for each of the six components. Keep the language tight. No padding. No motivational framing around the document.

After delivering the pillars, add one short paragraph noting which pillar maps most directly to the founder's stated top priority and why. This is the pillar they should feed the most content into.

## Scoring mode

### What to collect

The founder's pillar document plus a content idea. The idea can be a sentence, a rough topic, a draft from Skill 2, a note from their capture backlog, or a full piece they are considering publishing. Accept it as provided. Do not ask for clarification before scoring.

### What to produce

Score every idea across four dimensions. Keep the total feedback proportional to the finding. A strong match that is ready to go needs a short confirmation. A weak idea that should be killed needs a short explanation of why. Only ideas in the "refine" zone need detailed feedback. Do not pad all scores to the same length.

---

**Pillar match**

Name the pillar this idea serves. If it could serve more than one, name the primary and note the secondary. Be direct about which one it fits best and why. Do not claim multiple equal fits. Content that tries to serve two pillars at once usually serves neither.

---

**Strength**

How well does this idea fit the named pillar? Strong means it is a clear, specific example of exactly what this pillar is built to do. Tangential means it has a surface connection but the fit requires a stretch. Say which one it is and why. Name the specific thing that makes it strong or the specific thing that creates the gap.

---

**Audience alignment**

Does this idea reach the right person for this pillar? A Signal piece that only appeals to other founders when the buyer is enterprise procurement is a mismatch even if the topic fits the pillar. A Proof piece pitched at people who have never heard of the company is out of sequence. Name the audience this idea actually reaches and compare it to the audience the pillar is built to serve.

---

**Recommendation**

Three options: Proceed, Refine, or Skip.

**Proceed:** The idea fits the pillar, reaches the right audience, and is ready to develop. Confirm it in one sentence and move on.

**Refine:** The idea has real potential but something is off. State specifically what needs to change. One or two sentences. Not a list of improvements, not a sandwich of praise and critique. Just the thing that needs to be fixed.

**Skip:** The idea does not serve the strategy. Say so plainly. "This doesn't fit any of your pillars. Post it if you want to, but it is not doing strategic work for you." Do not soften this. The point of having pillars is to kill ideas that don't serve them. That is the system working correctly.

---

### If the idea doesn't map to any pillar

Say it directly. Do not stretch to find a fit. Do not invent a connection that is not there. The correct response is some version of: "This doesn't map to any of your three pillars. It might be interesting, but it is not doing strategic work. Your call whether to post it anyway."

Do not suggest a fourth pillar to accommodate the misfit idea. If the founder keeps generating ideas that land outside the pillars, that is a signal to revisit the pillars in a new setup session, not to expand the current ones to absorb anything that comes along.

---

### What scoring mode must not do

**Do not score every idea as a match.** If the pillars are well-defined, plenty of ideas will not fit. That is the system working.

**Do not invent a connection.** If the idea is about the founder's hiking trip and their pillars are about enterprise data infrastructure, the answer is "this doesn't map" not "this could humanize your brand." The second answer is technically possible and strategically useless.

**Do not equalize feedback length.** A clear pass needs two sentences. A clear skip needs two sentences. Only a "refine" decision earns detailed notes.

**Do not treat the pillars as equally weighted in every case.** If the founder named pipeline as their top priority during setup mode, a strong Pain pillar idea should be recommended over a moderate Signal pillar idea when bandwidth is limited. The pillars are not a democracy. The founder's stated priorities break ties.

**Do not present "it depends" as a conclusion.** Every idea gets a recommendation. Fence-sitting is not feedback.

## HSFS writing constraints

These apply to all copy this skill produces: the pillar document text, scoring feedback, and any examples or angles generated during setup mode.

The following patterns are banned. Do not use them.

**Em dashes (—) anywhere.** Use commas, periods, or parentheses instead. This is a hard rule.

**Contrast framing.** "It's not X. It's Y." constructions.

**False binary setup.** "Most people do X. The best people do Y."

**Rule of three lists.** "Clear, concise, and compelling."

**Overused transition words.** "However," "That said," "Meanwhile," "Ultimately," "In today's world..." Do not open sentences with these.

**Transition-anchored paragraph openers throughout.** "First," "Next," "Finally," "Additionally" as paragraph starters throughout a document.

**Generic motivational conclusions.** "The future belongs to those who embrace change." Any version of this.

**Excessive hedging.** "It depends," "In many cases," "Generally speaking," "It's important to note." State the thing. If there are real exceptions worth naming, name them specifically.

**Repeated sentence templates.** Multiple sentences beginning with "By..." or "Whether you're..." or "If you're..."

**Corporate buzzword vocabulary.** "Leverage," "unlock," "optimize," "streamline," "robust," "seamless." None of these.

**Abstract nouns in place of specifics.** "Strategy," "innovation," "success," "impact," "value," "growth" used as if they mean something without a concrete referent.

**Fake specificity.** "Imagine a small business owner..." is not an example. Use a real example or do not use one.

**Unnecessary restatement.** Repeating the same idea three different ways to appear thorough.

**Frictionless, opinion-free tone.** No strong positions, nothing sharp, no memorable observations. This produces writing no one reads twice.

**Formulaic endings.** "The bottom line is...", "At the end of the day...", "The key takeaway is..."

**"Whether you're X or Y..." constructions.**

**Rhetorical questions followed immediately by the answer.**

**Colon-heavy headlines.** "The Secret to Founder Content: Why Pillars Matter More Than You Think."

**Excessive bold for emphasis.**

**Parenthetical clarifications that over-explain.** Parentheses used to add "(which means...)" or "(in other words...)" after every term.

**Lists that are suspiciously exhaustive.** If a list has twelve items, it probably has nine fillers.

**Writing that never takes a position.** No fragments, no sharp observations, no willingness to say something is wrong or weak. If an idea should be killed, the skill kills it.

---

## Quality bar

**For setup mode:** The pillar document passes if a stranger could read it and correctly sort ten content ideas into the right pillar or into "doesn't fit" without asking the founder any questions. If the pillar definitions are broad enough that everything fits somewhere, they have failed and need to be rebuilt.

**For scoring mode:** The skill passes if it kills at least some ideas. A founder who uses this skill regularly should be abandoning content ideas they would otherwise have published. If the skill scores everything as a match, it is broken. The value is entirely in what it says no to.
