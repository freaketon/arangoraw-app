# ArangoRAW Content Pillars

Built with Alejandro Arango on September 25, 2026. Replaces the six app pillars (Psychology of Chaos, Media Intelligence, Identity Shift, Physics of Business, The Survivor, External Mirrors) and the four pillars in the 2026 Reels Strategy.

## Positioning

**Tagline.** Real stories. Wild comebacks. Your turn.

**Mission line.** Be the founder of your own life.

**Who it's for.** People who never felt the system was built for them. They've been trying and struggling, they want to reach a different level, and they want creative, unusual ways to get there. Founders of their own life, whether or not they run a company, broke or billionaire.

**Roles.** Serial entrepreneur. Creative director. Storyteller. Never introduce him by nationality or city.

**Who Alejo is on screen.** The acting force, shown and never claimed. He never calls himself a force of nature, a game changer or anything like it. The content makes people say it about him. The philosophy lives in the message. He doesn't play the philosopher.

**What content has to bring in.** Speaking gigs, book readers, community members. Client work is out of scope.

---

## Pillar 1: Real Stories, Wild Comebacks

**Description.** True stories told fast and visual like movie scenes. Two sources feed it. Alejo's own life from the book and the story library (Medellín, Canada, the army, film, Contento, the takeover, Mowies, the hospital, leaving Colombia). And stories he goes looking for: role models, other people's comebacks, big human stories that connect to something he lived. When a story is someone else's, it's paired with a moment from his own life, so the connection is his and nobody else's. Every piece is one scene with one turn, and it ends on what the story made possible.

**Audience.** Strangers scrolling. They don't know Alejo and aren't looking for help. They stay because the story is too good to skip. Cold, top of funnel.

**Business outcome.** Audience growth and book readers. The stories are also the raw material for keynotes.

**Example topics, his own.**
- One day old, and a gun already pointed at him. His dad disappeared for a month.
- In the army, he's picked to be the translator for a US colonel. Three weeks in the jungle, shot at, and he knocks down a wall at Pablo Escobar's jail with a grenade launcher.
- A new kid in Mississauga who can't speak English trains a hardflip in his basement for months, lands it once at the mall, and becomes "Ali."
- Lost everything, crying from 10 PM to 4 AM, he tells God "don't count with me" until he gets a clear message. The phone rings. "You are a creator."

**Example topics, other people's stories paired with his** (facts to verify before production).
- Steve Jobs pushed out of the company he founded in 1985, back in 1997. Paired with Alejo losing Contento in a hostile takeover.
- Temple Grandin, autistic, built a career out of seeing what others couldn't. Paired with Alejo's AuDHD diagnosis and how it changed the way he builds.
- Casey Neistat, a dad at 17 washing dishes, before YouTube. Paired with Alejo quitting at 19 with $200.

**Rules for other people's stories.**
- Facts are researched and checked, with the source kept in the story library. No invented details, dialogue or motives.
- Always connect back to Alejo's life. A retold famous story with no link to him belongs to anyone and does nothing for ArangoRAW.

**What does NOT belong here.**
- A lesson with no scene. If there's no place, no person and no moment, it goes somewhere else or nowhere.
- Violence or trauma for shock with no turn.
- Celebrity stories used as generic motivation ("Oprah was poor too").
- Any of Alejo's stories that isn't in the book or the story library.

## Pillar 2: Your Turn

**Description.** Alejo teaching the creative, unusual ways he rebuilds himself and his life, always grounded in something he actually did. His AuDHD brain as the operating system. Naming his devil Milton. Starting from zero again. Building a life around what he loves and cutting what he hates. Using AI so one mind can do what used to take hundreds of people. Each piece hands the viewer one thing to try. Be the founder of your own life.

**Audience.** The person who's been trying and struggling and feels the system wasn't made for them. They've seen a few stories and now want to know how. Warm, middle of funnel.

**Business outcome.** Community members. This pillar is a preview of what the community gets.

**Example topics.**
- He named the darkness in his head Milton, after *Outwitting the Devil*. When it shows up at 3 AM he says "there's Milton again." How naming your devil takes away its power.
- His hate list (fundraising, admin, legal) and his love list (creating, building, marketing). He rebuilt his work around the second one.
- Starting over for the fourth time in Vancouver, where his whole history meant nothing. "Nothing has never stopped me. It's just the starting position."
- MidJourney felt like the starting gun: one creative mind can now do what took millions of dollars and hundreds of people.

**What does NOT belong here.**
- Generic productivity or morning-routine tips.
- Money listicles, get-rich promises, "100 ways to..." content.
- Named frameworks with no story behind them ("The Energy Audit").
- AI tool tutorials that could come from anyone.

## Pillar 3: In Motion

**Description.** Alejo building right now, Casey Neistat style. Behind the scenes of whatever he's shooting, writing or speaking at this week. The book in progress. Talks and stages. Videos he makes alone with AI. Live proof that the guy in the stories is still doing it. The viewer sees the energy and names it themselves.

**Audience.** People deciding whether he's the real thing: event organizers, podcast hosts, and followers close to joining the community.

**Business outcome.** Speaking gigs, plus trust that turns followers into community members.

**Example topics.**
- The run-up to the October 1 Digital Marketing Summit talk: building it, rehearsing it, backstage, and what happened on stage.
- One day, one creator, one film: making a full piece alone with AI, start to finish.
- Building the Wonder Years book: the manuscript, the editorial system, the scenes that almost didn't make it.
- "Where my time went": a real week, shown on screen.

**What does NOT belong here.**
- Self-labels ("force of nature," "game changer," "unstoppable").
- Polished announcements ("excited to share," "humbled to announce").
- Client work showcases.
- Hype about what he's going to do with nothing made yet.

---

## Which pillar to feed most

Real Stories, Wild Comebacks. His own stories are the one thing nobody can copy, and the stories he brings in widen who connects. It builds the audience the other two need and feeds book readers and keynotes directly. A rough weekly split: 50% Real Stories, 30% Your Turn, 20% In Motion. Around a talk or a book launch, In Motion goes up.
